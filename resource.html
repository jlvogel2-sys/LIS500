What is Intersectionality?

Intersectionality is the understanding that our various social identities, such as race, gender, socio-economic class, sexuality, and ability, do not exist in isolation. Instead, these identities constantly interact with one another. This shapes how each individual experiences the world differently. Broad categories don't always capture the unique challenges or strengths of overlapping identities, which is why specialized groups and resources are so essential.

According to Patricia Hill Collins, intersectionality is a way of understanding and analyzing the complexity in the world, in people, and in human experiences. The events and conditions of social and political life and the self can seldom be understood as shaped by one factor. They are generally shaped by many factors in diverse and mutually influencing ways. When it comes to social inequality, people's lives and the organization of power in a given society are better understood as being shaped not by a single axis of social division, be it race or gender or class, but by many axes that work together and influence each other. Intersectionality as an analytic tool gives people better access to the complexity of the world and of themselves.

Intersectionality as a framework for analyzing how power operates across and through these interconnected axes.

Intersectionality can also be used to understand how power operates across these interconnected axes. Social structures in our society do not distribute power, privilege, and marginalization along isolated tracks. Instead, systems can compound and reinforce one another. When institutions operate with a single-axis perspective, they often obscure how institutional power privileges certain groups while compounding barriers for others.
