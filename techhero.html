Dr. Rediet Abebe

Dr. Rediet Abebe is a computer scientist specializing in algorithms and artificial intelligence. She was born and raised in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. She is the first Black woman to earn a Ph.D. in Computer Science from Cornell! She is currently an assistant professor of computer science at the University of California, Berkeley.

She co-founded Mechanism Design for Social Good (MD4SG) alongside Kira Goldner in 2016. The goals of MD4SG is to use algorithms and mechanism design to tackle inequality. Her work with MD4SG had her honored as a pioneer in the 2019 MIT Technology Review's Innovators Under 35. She also co-founded Black in AI, a network of 1,500 researchers working on AI, with Timnit Gebru, in 2016.

Dr. Timnit Gebru

Josie's tech hero: Dr. Timnit Gebru is a computer scientist and researcher specializing in artificial intelligence ethics, algorithmic bias, and computer vision. After her education at Stanford University, Gebru has held influential positions at Apple, Microsoft Research, and Google, where she co-led the Ethical Artificial Intelligence team. I think one of her most influential aspects is her co-author position on the research paper Gender Shades. In her research she exposed significant racial and gender disparities in commercial facial recognition systems, demonstrating how algorithmic models systematically fail on dark-skinned women. Gebru introduced an intersectional methodology to audit these models, analyzing how accuracy collapsed when race and gender overlapped. The study revealed that while commercial systems performed near-perfectly on light-skinned men, their error rates skyrocketed to nearly 35% for darker-skinned women.

Gebru has criticized the lack of diversity in Silicon Valley, specifically with the exclusion of Black women in the development of AI. This matters because it has the potential to produce harmful, exclusionary software. I chose to write about Dr. Timnit Gebru as my tech hero because she has changed how I think about the role of technology in society. I found it really inspirational how she was able to challenge the industry that she works for, which many feel they cannot do. Beyond her research, her willingness to speak the truth about how unchecked systems can systemically affect marginalized groups makes her an inspirational role model.

Recently her research looks at how the entire supply chain of AI affects humans. She is looking at how tech companies often hide exploited labor behind AI. She advocates for the data labelers and content moderators doing painful, underpaid work, and even exposes new forms of forced labor where "zombie trainers" generate AI data without their knowledge.
